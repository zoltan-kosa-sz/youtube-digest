Three-layer framework for structured AI collaboration

2026-09-20 / Watch

Use

  1. Identify your actual goal before asking Claude to build — Have Claude interview you to uncover what decision or conclusion you're trying to reach, not just the task you want done.1:34
  1. Define evaluation criteria in your Claude.md before any work starts — Write what "good" looks like with precision (not "make it better," but "three sections, each ending with a recommendation"). Verification feedback loops improve output quality 2–3x.6:13
  1. Create a knowledge base and custom skills to reuse — Set up a folder structure on your machine for data Claude should know about, and write skills for repeated tasks so your system compounds over time instead of resetting each session.10:24

Bottom line

AI fails at context-driven tasks not from stupidity but from lack of signal — it can't know what you need. Bridge this gap with three layers: a precise spec (clarifying your goal), a verification checkpoint (checking the work), and a persistent environment (your Claude.md + knowledge base + skills) that improves with use.

Key ideas

  • AI excels at measurable tasks, stumbles at context-driven ones — asking Claude whether to drive or walk to a car wash confuses it because it can measure distance but can't infer that the car itself must reach the destination.0:31
  • A spec is how you deliver your understanding to Claude in a usable format — uncover the actual goal, break work into small reviewable chunks (agile, not waterfall), and stay precise so Claude has less room to drift.1:02
  • Treat AI as a robot librarian with a finite library, not a person with motivation — it can only suggest answers from its training data and doesn't know when it's confidently making something up. Yelling at it doesn't help; only the verification lever does.5:10
  • Define evaluation criteria upfront before Claude touches a single thing — vague standards ("make this look good") guarantee failure; precise ones ("three sections, each with a recommendation") cut the room for misalignment.6:13
  • Use a second AI model to critique the first one's output — another LLM has different training data and may catch errors the first one missed; in Claude Code, run output past Codex to ensure both systems agree.6:45
  • Set up Claude.md as your persistent standing instructions — it gets injected before every session, so outline your repo structure, where Claude should look for information, custom skills, and rule-based guardrails (enforced via pre-tool hooks, not prompt wishes).9:22
  • You can outsource thinking but not understanding — the three layers all depend on your clarity about goals, evaluation criteria, and what matters in your domain.12:57
