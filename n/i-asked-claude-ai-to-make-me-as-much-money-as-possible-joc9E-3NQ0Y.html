I Asked Claude AI To Make Me As Much Money As Possible

Shane Hummus / 2026-09-11 / Watch

Use

  1. When prompting Claude for income ideas, have it interview you about your existing career/skills/hobbies first — a bare "make me money" prompt returns a generic, traffic-dependent list (dropshipping, print-on-demand, faceless channel), not a personalized plan.
  2. Choosing how to monetize a new audience: pick affiliate marketing or a service (sell before building) over a course/software (build before selling) — faster path to first revenue.
  3. Structure any content business around one long-form YouTube video/week, repurposed into shorts/clips/posts elsewhere, capped at a few hours/week — not separate content per platform.

Bottom line

Claude doesn't invent profitable ideas from nothing — a vague prompt returns a generic list that needs an audience you probably don't have. The actual leverage is using it to package expertise you already have into content plus an affiliate/service offer; the video's "AI made me money" framing obscures that audience-building on YouTube, not the model, does the real work.

Key ideas

  • Garbage in, garbage out applies to business prompts — "make me as much money as possible" returns the same generic list every time regardless of who's asking; those options need traffic most beginners don't have.
  • "Mile 23": expertise that feels effortless to you doesn't register as a skill — people undervalue what's second nature and chase unfamiliar "easy-looking" ventures instead, which actually take thousands of hours to reach the same competence.
  • Three ways to get buyers: paid ads, cold outreach, content — content compounds and keeps working after it's made; ads and outreach require continuous spend or effort per lead.
  • Affiliate or service beats course or software for time-to-first-dollar — a course/software needs months of build-out before any revenue; affiliate and service models can be sold before (or without) being built.
  • One "mother platform," syndicated everywhere — one long-form YouTube video gets repurposed into shorts, clips, posts, emails automatically rather than creating content separately per platform.

Reality check

  • MixedCourse Careers self-reported outcomes ($42k→$58.7k average salary jump, high job-placement rate) — figures roughly match Course Careers' own 2025 Outcomes Report, but it's self-reported survey data from a company the speaker is paid to promote as an affiliate, not independently audited.
  • Mixed"4.7 excellent score on Trustpilot" for Course Careers — current Trustpilot rating is closer to 4.0–4.2 stars (422 reviews); the 4.7 figure appears inflated versus the primary source, though other review sites (Career Karma, 4.8) run higher.
  • MixedDunning-Kruger effect cited to explain why people underrate familiar skills and overrate unfamiliar ones — the actual DKE describes low performers overestimating their own test performance on a skill they lack, not general perceived-ease of an unfamiliar business venture; this is a common but loose pop-psychology extension of the term.
  • MixedIndividual income claims (Zach $18k/mo, Nurse Jen $10k+/mo, Josh $185k/mo, Shawn Dolit $500k/mo, etc.) — all self-reported, screenshot-sourced testimonials from the speaker's own paid coaching community, not third-party verified; presented as "receipts" but function as survivorship-biased proof for the free live-training / 1:1-coaching funnel the video builds toward throughout.
