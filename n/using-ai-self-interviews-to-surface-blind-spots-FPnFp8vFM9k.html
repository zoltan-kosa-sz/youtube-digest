Using AI self-interviews to surface blind spots

Sandeep Swadia / 2026-09-11 / Watch

Use

  1. Pick one recurring pattern you can't explain (self-sabotage, conflict, a stuck decision, an identity shift, or unresolved pain) and run the matching prompt below with AI — one blind spot, one session.
  2. Before a hard conversation, have AI argue the other person's side first (voice mode), so you rehearse instead of relitigating your own story.
  3. Before a big irreversible decision, run a pre-mortem with AI on each option separately, then apply Bezos' one-way/two-way door test to decide how fast to move.

Bottom line

AI can't tell you the truth about yourself, but it can interview you rigorously enough to surface the story you've been protecting — treat every output as a hypothesis to test against real behavior and real feedback, not a diagnosis.

Key ideas

  • Five blind spots, five AI roles: self-deception (AI as investigator), relationship stories (AI as the other party), identity change (AI as mapmaker), hard decisions (AI as pre-mortem simulator), trauma/failure (AI as debrief partner — not therapist).
  • Self-deception persists because feedback, not memory, corrects it. A pattern of behavior only breaks once someone who knows you gives concrete negative feedback more than once — social-media criticism doesn't count, and neither does introspection alone.
  • Every conversation has four layers: what you meant, what you said, what they heard, what they interpreted — conflict escalates when new details just get recruited to confirm a fixed story about the other person.
  • Replace accusation with observation: name the behavior, name its impact on you, then ask a genuine question — instead of "you don't care," say "when I don't hear back for 2 days I wonder if this matters to you — am I being fair?"
  • You keep changing whether you expect to or not ("end of history illusion") — people accurately recall how much they've changed but wrongly assume they've now arrived at a final self, which makes reinvention feel like an identity crisis instead of a navigation problem.
  • Pre-mortem before committing: imagine the decision failed badly two years out and work backward — for each option, identify the likely failure mode, the early warning signs, and the cost that can't be undone.
  • Writing about a painful experience for 15 min/day over ~4 days measurably improves health months later — naming facts vs. the meaning you've attached to them, and what was/wasn't in your control, does real work that comforting self-talk doesn't.

Reality check

  • Supportedthe "end of history illusion" (people underestimate future change) — real study, Quoidbach/Gilbert/Wilson 2013, Science, 19,000+ respondents ages 18–68, matches the video's description closely.
  • Supportedexpressive writing (15 min/day, ~4 days) improves later health outcomes — Pennebaker's original protocol and its many replications show reduced doctor visits and better long-term health measures; well-established.
  • Supportednegative feedback reduces self-deception — a 2019 Frontiers in Psychology study found real (not ambiguous) negative feedback measurably decreases self-deception, consistent with the claim.
  • Supportedathletes whose identity fuses with their sport struggle more after retirement — well-documented in sport psychology as "athletic identity foreclosure."
  • Mixedthe Eli Finkel/Northwestern couples study — real (120 couples, Psychological Science 2013), but the video compresses it: the actual intervention was three separate 7-minute writing sessions (21 min total) spread across a year, not one ~20-minute sitting, and it *slowed* the decline in relationship quality relative to the control group rather than stopping decline outright for "those couples" as a single event.
  • Mixed"840,000 crashes a year... involve lane changes and blind spots," framed around 18-wheelers — the 840,000 figure is for blind-spot-related crashes across all vehicles, not trucks specifically; large-truck lane-change crashes are a much smaller subset (~97,000/year per industry estimates). The number is directionally real but the truck framing overstates its relevance.
