AI's trillion-dollar business case doesn't add up

TEDxBerlin / 2026-09-20 / Watch

Use

  1. Recognize unrealistic valuation claims — most AI company financial projections depend on implausible global GDP growth (26% annually) while their own economists predict 2–4%, the historical average.
  2. Evaluate IPO structures as wealth extraction — SpaceX's 4.2% float, 60-day lockup, and 85% founder control are designed to enrich early investors while passive funds are forced to buy, not to provide actual returns to public investors.
  3. Understand why companies keep spending despite losses — the real goal is reaching IPO exit dates, not building sustainable businesses; Financial Times found four of five major labs would be loss-making even with zero operating costs.

Bottom line

The AI industry's $10+ trillion revenue projections are built on fictional GDP growth targets; companies keep scaling because the actual business model is funneling VC money toward IPO exits for founders and early investors, not creating value. The race between US and China is not about geopolitical capability—it's about who gets liquidity first.

Key ideas

  • Inference costs drop 40x per year on average — even at 9x-900x range depending on task, this deflationary spiral undercuts any datacenters-as-revenue-engine model.7:15
  • Chinese open-source models already dominate usage — six of ten most popular models in developer use are Chinese, not from major labs, because they cost vastly less and work well enough.7:47
  • Four of five major AI companies are already loss-making — even before operating costs; datacenter deployment costs have quadrupled ($10–15B per gigawatt in 2020 → $40–50B now), while pricing collapses.3:06
  • The real race is VCs pushing toward exits, not geopolitics — founders' incentives align with reaching IPO, not with building AGI; the framing as geopolitical competition masks a pure financial extraction play.10:25
  • K-shaped economy already visible — stock market up since ChatGPT, but job listings down; youth unemployment 9% vs 4.3% general, underemployment 42% for recent graduates.15:06
  • AI will likely cause deflation, not growth — as automation makes goods cheaper, overall economy may shrink but living costs fall; this contradicts promised 26% annual GDP growth.6:44
  • Solutions require policy intervention — treat AI as public good, regulate for safety, slow rollout to let society adapt (compare 5–10 years vs 40–80 years for prior revolutions), deploy social safety nets, prevent misuse via global institutions.18:11

  • SupportedErica Chenoweth's 3.5% rule — confirmed 2011 research on 323 campaigns (1900–2006); nonviolent movements reaching 3.5% participation succeeded. Caveat: requires sustained action (strikes, boycotts) over months/years, not flash protests. [Wikipedia: 3.5% rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.5%_rule)
  • SupportedUS youth unemployment ~9%, underemployment ~42% — May 2026 data: 9.4% youth unemployment vs 4.3% general; Q4 2025 underemployment for recent grads at 42.5%. [Trading Economics, Federal Reserve Bank of New York](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/youth-unemployment-rate)
  • SupportedSpaceX IPO structure: $1.75T valuation, 4.2% float, 60-day lockup, Elon retains 85% control — confirmed June 2026 IPO at $1.77T valuation; 4.2% share sold; listed June 12, 2026. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-raises-75-billion-in-record-setting-ipo-ahead-of-nasdaq-debut.html)
