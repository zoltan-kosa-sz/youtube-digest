15 UI/UX mistakes to fix on product pages

2026-09-20 / Watch

Use

  1. Audit your product page against these 15 concrete mistakes — icons over images, product photo selection, grid alignment, color saturation, typography, label density, title hierarchy, paragraph spacing, review placement, feature icons, dividers, spacing, redundant labels, price placement, and button behavior. This works whether you're designing ecommerce or any interface.
  2. Apply grid-based design as a foundation — consistent margins and alignment make every interface feel more intentional and trustworthy, even when users don't consciously notice misalignment.
  3. Test improvements against user behavior, not aesthetics — sticky action buttons, predefined quantity options, and product title in navigation all solve real friction points. Use purchase data to decide which options to show.

Bottom line

Good UI is not about making things look beautiful in isolation — it's about understanding why each decision makes the product easier to use, trust, and buy from. Most tutorials show what to fix; this shows why it matters for the user's actual journey.

Key ideas

  • Icons need background containers to work across any product image — overlaying icons directly on product photos fails when the image is bright. A subtle background behind icons ensures 3:1 contrast regardless of what's underneath.0:31
  • Product images must serve the system, not just look beautiful alone — when every product in a catalog has different styling, lighting, and background, the whole interface feels fragmented. Consistency across products matters more than individual beauty.2:05
  • Consistent grid alignment sets the mood of a page — when text, prices, and sections snap to the same margins, the interface feels structured and trustworthy. Users feel misalignment even when they don't consciously see it.3:38
  • Color should support the product, not compete for attention — oversaturated or too-bright colors make text harder to read and weaken visual hierarchy. Softer palettes let the product become the focus.4:39
  • One font family with hierarchy through size, weight, and line height — too many fonts fragment the design. Use a single family and create hierarchy through sizing and weight instead.5:09
  • Place rating near the product title — "What is it?" and "Can I trust it?" are the same question in the buyer's mind. Putting reviews close to the product name answers both at once.7:14
  • Sticky action buttons and predefined quantity options remove friction — users don't always decide to buy at the top of the page. Keeping the add-to-cart button visible while scrolling, plus one-tap quantity presets based on purchase data, removes decision friction.12:26

Reality check

  • SupportedReviews are one of the strongest trust signals in ecommerce — 98% of consumers read reviews, 79% trust them as much as personal recommendations, and star ratings near product titles drive measurable conversion lift. Placing reviews near the product name aligns with current ecommerce best practice.
  • SupportedIcon contrast requirements (WCAG 2.1 standard) specify icons must have 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors. Adding background containers behind icons to ensure visibility across different product images is both an accessibility and usability best practice.
