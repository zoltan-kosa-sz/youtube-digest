Publishing niche ebooks with Claude and Kindle data

Sean Dollwet / 2026-09-11 / Watch

Use

  1. Before writing anything, validate demand: search Amazon for the topic, check best-seller-rank/sales-estimate data (co-author.ai Chrome extension, or manually via product page) for existing books — only proceed if others are already selling on that topic.
  2. If testing this as an experiment, budget it as a $0–50 low-stakes trial (free KDP listing, free Canva cover, optional co-author.ai/Book Bounty subscriptions) and treat any income as a bonus, not an expectation — real KDP earnings data (below) is far below what the video implies.
  3. Don't buy the "KDP mentorship program" pitched at the end without separately verifying its cost and the coach's actual track record — the whole video functions as a funnel toward it and toward paid tools (co-author.ai, Book Bounty).

Bottom line

The core method — check Amazon sales data before creating a niche non-fiction ebook, draft it section-by-section with Claude, list free on Kindle Direct Publishing — is a legitimate, low-cost way to test a content-product idea. The video's income claims ($2.3M personal earnings, an $820K account sale, a student's $161K first year) are unverifiable and far above typical KDP results.

Key ideas

  • Three-step model: find a niche that already sells on Amazon, make a shorter/simpler version, list it where buyers already search — no website, ads, or funnel needed; Amazon's own traffic replaces marketing.
  • Validate with data, not intuition — use Amazon best-seller-rank (a proxy for sales volume) to confirm demand for a topic before spending time writing it; no sales on the topic = no proof of demand.
  • Non-fiction value is "solved my problem fast," not page count — a focused 10,000–30,000-word book aimed at one clear pain point can outsell a 300-page book on the same topic.
  • Generate content one section at a time, not whole chapters — asking an LLM for large chunks in one shot increases repetition and surface-level content; edit and add personal detail afterward rather than publishing raw output.
  • A handful of reviews (5+) meaningfully improves conversion for a new listing with zero social proof; get them from strangers (free copies in relevant Facebook groups, or paid services like Book Bounty) — reviews from friends/family violate Amazon's terms.
  • Scaling is more/better books, a team, ads, and format expansion (paperback/audiobook) — not a different mechanism than the first sale.

Reality check

  • Mixed"more than 70% of all US book sales happen on Amazon" — plausible for ebooks specifically (~68%, up to ~83% with Kindle Unlimited) but overall book-sales share estimates across sources range roughly 40–83% depending on format; there's no single settled 70% figure for "all books."
  • Supported, but overstated"book sales are increasing year after year" — true for the last two years (print units up ~0.6% in 2024, ~0.3% in 2025 per Circana BookScan), but growth is marginal and still below the 2021 peak — not the boom the framing implies.
  • Unverifiable"I made over $2.3 million doing this" and "sold a KDP account for $820,000" — no independent evidence for either; presented as authority-building right before pitching a paid mentorship program, a classic guru-credibility pattern.
  • Cherry-picked, not typical — student testimonials ($40 in one day, $161K first year from 10 books) are flagged by the speaker himself as "not typical," yet used as proof the model works. Independent data on KDP puts the median author around $1,000/year total, with ~80% of authors who have 1–3 books earning under $100/month — the realistic outcome is much closer to the video's own disclaimer than to the testimonials shown.
